# Credit union and nonprofit/CDFI small-dollar loan options for Texas residents with sub-580 credit (loan of $1,000+), as of November 2026

Research method note (read first): this session could only use web search; direct page fetches of lender sites (capitalgoodfund.org, bcloftexas.org, myncu.com) were blocked by the session's network allowlist. Every finding below therefore rests on search-result excerpts of the linked pages, not on a full read of the page. Figures marked "unverified" or "older source" must be confirmed on the lender's own page before being stated as current. No finding here is a guarantee of approval for the borrower profile (FICO under 580, about $5,300-$6,000/month job income, possibly only ~3 months at that income).

## 1. What are the PAL I and PAL II rules (amounts, 28% APR cap, membership length, fees) and which Texas credit unions actually offer them?

### Takeaway
PALs are a federal-credit-union-only product capped at 28% APR plus a $20 application fee; PAL I is $200-$1,000 (1-6 months, one month of membership required) and PAL II is up to $2,000 (1-12 months, no membership waiting period under the federal rule). Only a few Texas credit unions were confirmed as offering PAL-type loans, and those found mostly impose their own longer membership waits (6-12 months) or cap the loan at $500, so a brand-new member generally cannot get $1,000 quickly this way.

### Cited Findings
- NCUA's guidance table lists a $20 maximum application fee for both PAL I and PAL II — [NCUA letter to credit unions](https://ncua.gov/regulation-supervision/letters-credit-unions-other-guidance/cfpb-issues-amendments-payday-vehicle-title-and-certain-high-cost-installment-loans-rule)
- PAL I: $200 to $1,000, terms of 1 to 6 months, borrower must have been a member at least one month. PAL II: up to $2,000, terms of 1 to 12 months, no membership waiting period; finalized by NCUA in 2019 — [Gerald summary of PAL rules](https://joingerald.com/learn/cash-advance/payday-alternatives-disclosure-rules) (secondary, vendor content); PAL I range and term also stated by the federal consumer site [MyCreditUnion.gov](https://mycreditunion.gov/manage-your-money/consumer-loans-credit-cards/payday-alternative-loans)
- 28% rate ceiling applies to both PAL versions — [Gerald](https://joingerald.com/learn/debt--credit/pal-loan); [NerdWallet PAL explainer](https://www.nerdwallet.com/personal-loans/learn/payday-alternative-loan-pal)
- A member may hold only one PAL at a time; a credit union's total PAL balances are capped at 20% of its net worth — [Financial Services Perspectives (2019)](https://www.financialservicesperspectives.com/2019/10/the-ncua-doubles-amount-credit-unions-can-offer-for-payday-alternative-loans/)
- No more than three PALs to one borrower in any rolling six-month period — [LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/payday-alternative-loan/)
- No rollovers; a PAL must be fully repaid by the end of its term — [Money Crashers](https://www.moneycrashers.com/payday-alternative-loan/)
- Credit checks on PALs vary: a credit union may run a hard inquiry — [Credible](https://credible.com/personal-loan/payday-alternative-loan)
- PALs are available only from federal credit unions; state-chartered credit unions (example given: Texans Credit Union) are not bound by PAL terms and may differ — [Gerald](https://joingerald.com/learn/cash-advance/texanscu-cash-advance-alternatives-2026) (vendor content, low authority)
- Credit Human (San Antonio; federal charter) "QMoney" loan: $200 to $500, 60 days to repay, no credit check, $20 application fee; eligibility requires being a Credit Human member, primary account holder, with a deposit account open 6 months or more, age 18+. Membership requires a share savings account with a $5 minimum deposit — [Credit Human QMoney page](https://credithuman.com/personal/personal-loans/qmoney-loan/); [Credit Human membership](https://credithuman.com/who-we-serve/). Rate table reportedly shows 28.00% for the 60-day QMoney loan, with an effective date of September 20, 2023 (older) — [Credit Human personal loans](https://credithuman.com/borrow/personal-loans)
- Texas Plains Federal Credit Union: PAL-style loan up to $2,000, up to 12 months, 24% interest, $20 application fee, no credit check, instant decision, but requires 10 months of credit union membership — [Texas Plains FCU unsecured loans](https://www.texasplainsfederal.org/loans/unsecured-loans/)
- Neighborhood Credit Union (Dallas area) "Quick Cash" loan: up to $2,000, but requires 12 consecutive months of membership — [Neighborhood CU Quick Cash](https://myncu.com/borrow/quick-cash-loan)
- GECU (El Paso; federal credit union) is listed in a directory as offering Payday Alternative Loans, and a CDFI case study says it expanded "payday lending alternatives, including Small Dollar Loans and Credit Builder products" — [ncuso.org directory](https://ncuso.org/credit-union/24941/); [CDFI case study (2024)](https://cdfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/case_46.pdf). A consumer review site cites a 27.90% personal loan rate and a $500-$1,000 credit-builder loan held in a 12-month certificate — [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/finance/gecu-bank.html) (third-party, unverified against GECU)
- Navigator Credit Union small-dollar loan: $300 to $1,500, fixed 25% APR, no credit check — [Navigator CU](https://navigatorcu.org/loans/small-dollar-loan/). Search did not confirm that Navigator serves Texas (it appeared in a Texas-themed search but its field of membership was not shown).

### Inferences
- Under the federal rule a PAL II could in principle fund $1,000-$2,000 on the day of joining, but every Texas credit union PAL-type product found adds a stricter membership-tenure rule (6, 10, or 12 months) or a $500 cap. For a non-member needing $1,000 now, PALs are mostly a "join now, borrow later" option unless a specific federal credit union confirms same-day PAL II eligibility.
- PAL underwriting typically rests on membership and income/deposit history rather than score, which suits a sub-580 borrower with $5,300-$6,000/month income; the cost on $1,000 for 6 months at 28% plus $20 is far below Texas payday/CAB pricing.
- The Credit Human QMoney product does not meet the $1,000 minimum.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm from primary pages whether Randolph-Brooks FCU, Security Service FCU, Texas Dow Employees CU (TDECU), A+ FCU, University FCU, or Amplify offer a PAL or any no-credit-check small loan. Searches returned nothing reliable for a PAL at any of them; Amplify's own "alternatives to payday loans" content is a blog article, not a product, and no PAL appeared in its product listings ([Amplify blog](https://www.goamplify.com/blog/moneymanagement/payday-loan-alternatives/)).
- Did not read 12 CFR 701.21(c)(7)(iii)-(iv) directly; PAL rule details rely on NCUA guidance excerpts plus secondary sources. No source found indicating the rules changed through 2026, but that is absence of evidence, not confirmation.
- GECU's current PAL amount, membership-tenure rule, and fees were not found.
- No NCUA list of which Texas federal credit unions currently report PAL lending was retrieved (NCUA call report data would show this).

## 2. Which Texas credit unions or nonprofits lend $1,000 or more to members with sub-580 scores, and what are the membership, income, and job-tenure requirements?

### Takeaway
The realistically reachable $1,000+ options for a sub-580 Texan are: Capital Good Fund (nonprofit, no minimum FICO, Texas-eligible), a Community Loan Center loan if the employer participates (no credit history needed), Oportun (no minimum score, but APR up to about 36%), and Navy Federal if the borrower has a military/DoD tie (no published minimum score). Mainstream Texas credit union signature loans (e.g., RBFCU) appear to look for roughly fair credit and were not confirmed as available under 580.

### Cited Findings
**Nonprofit / CDFI lenders**
- Capital Good Fund Impact Loan: available in Texas (also RI, FL, DE, IL, NJ, CT); requirements listed as a bank account or approved prepaid card, residence in a participating state, income, two months of banking history via Plaid, government ID, voided check or ACH-capable prepaid card; states no minimum FICO requirement; zero application or closing costs; 15-month repayment — [Capital Good Fund Impact Loan overview](https://capitalgoodfund.org/impact-loan-overview/); [Capital Good Fund Texas page](https://capitalgoodfund.org/overview/impact-loans-tx-2/)
- United Way of Metropolitan Dallas describes partner small-dollar loans "up to $2,500," accessible to Texans with lower credit scores or no credit, APR not exceeding 35%, and a separate predatory-loan conversion loan up to $4,000 to pay off payday and auto title lenders; Capital Good Fund is the named lending partner — [United Way Dallas Affordable Loans](https://unitedwaydallas.org/resources/affordable-loans/). United Way reports 230 loans averaging $998 (over $224,000) in 2024-2025 through its Impact Loan Initiative — [United Way Dallas Impact Loan Initiative](https://unitedwaydallas.org/programs/impact-loan-initiative/)
- Same United Way page lists other partners: NOVA (credit access for immigrants without U.S. credit history), On the Road Lending (car loans), and Community Loan Center (employer-based small-dollar loans) — [United Way Dallas Affordable Loans](https://unitedwaydallas.org/resources/affordable-loans/)
- Community Loan Center (CLC): up to $1,000, 18% interest, $20 fee, up to 12 months, payroll deduction, "no credit history or collateral needed"; employee must be 18+ and have worked for a participating employer at least 3 months — [CLC of the Brazos Valley](https://clcbv.org/); [Rio Grande Valley CLC](http://www.rgvcommunityloancenter.com/); [City of Houston CLC FAQ](https://www.houstontx.gov/hr/hrfiles/benefits/FAQ-%20CLC.pdf) (see section 3)
- Oportun: no minimum credit score requirement — [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/finance/oportun.html); unsecured loans $300 to $10,000, APR cap 35.99%, with a commonly cited range of 19.9%-36%; 12 to 48 month terms; origination fees possible (other reviews say up to 10%); Texas is among states served, and secured loans (up to $18,500) are available in Texas; prequalification is a soft pull — [NerdWallet Oportun review](https://www.nerdwallet.com/personal-loans/reviews/oportun-personal-loans); [SuperMoney](https://www.supermoney.com/reviews/personal-loans/oportun-personal-loans); [Gerald](https://joingerald.com/learn/debt--credit/oportun-loans) (third-party sources; APR floor figures conflict)
- Mission Asset Fund: San Francisco nonprofit offering zero-interest lending circles; direct programs in California and delivered elsewhere through a national network of nonprofit partners — [Wikipedia: Mission Asset Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Asset_Fund)
- PeopleFund's 2025 annual report describes small-business and veteran lending (Texas member of the Veteran Loan Fund) and a partnership with a reentry-focused lender; no consumer personal loan product appeared — [PeopleFund Annual Report 2025](https://peoplefund.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/PeopleFund-Annual-Report-2025.pdf)
- BCL of Texas operates the Community Loan Centers of Dallas and Austin (employer-based, up to $1,000, 12 months, rate capped at 18%) — [BCL of Texas Loan Centers](https://bcloftexas.org/community/loan-centers)

**Credit unions**
- Navy Federal: personal loans start at $250; does not publish a minimum credit score; considers credit history and income; membership limited to military members, veterans, DoD employees and their families; commonly cited APR range 8.74%-18.00% — [NerdWallet Navy Federal 2026 review](https://www.nerdwallet.com/personal-loans/reviews/navy-federal-personal-loans); [LendingTree](https://lendingtree.com/personal/reviews/navy-federal); [Credible](https://www.credible.com/personal-loan/navy-federal-personal-loans-review)
- RBFCU: membership via living/working/worshiping/studying in listed Texas counties, employer, military or organizational affiliation, or family ties; membership is required to close a loan but an application can be started first — [RBFCU loans](https://www.rbfcu.org/loans); [Wikipedia: RBFCU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randolph-Brooks_Federal_Credit_Union). A third-party aggregator lists a minimum credit tier of "Fair (580-669)" and APRs of 8.50%-18.00% — [seeloans.com](https://www.seeloans.com/personal-loans/randolph-brooks-federal-credit-union-personal-loans-calculator/) (aggregator; not confirmed by RBFCU)
- Texas Plains FCU ($2,000, no credit check, 10 months' membership) and Neighborhood CU ($2,000 Quick Cash, 12 months' membership) reach $1,000+ without a score requirement but only for seasoned members — [Texas Plains FCU](https://www.texasplainsfederal.org/loans/unsecured-loans/); [Neighborhood CU](https://myncu.com/borrow/quick-cash-loan)
- Amplify Credit Union (Austin): personal loans up to 60-month terms, hard credit pull on application, possible origination fee — [SuperMoney Amplify review](https://www.supermoney.com/reviews/personal-loans/amplify-credit-union-personal-loans) (third-party)
- GECU credit-builder loan $500-$1,000 is deposited into a 12-month certificate (i.e., it does not provide cash up front) — [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/finance/gecu-bank.html)

**Large-bank small-dollar loans (existing customers only)**
- U.S. Bank Simple Loan: up to $1,000 in $100 increments, $6 fee per $100 borrowed, repaid in three monthly payments; existing-customer product — [NerdWallet: how bank loans work](https://www.nerdwallet.com/personal-loans/learn/how-do-bank-loans-work); [Credible U.S. Bank review](https://www.credible.com/personal-loan/us-bank-personal-loans-review). This is the only large-bank product found that clearly reaches $1,000.
- Bank of America Balance Assist: historically up to $500 for a $5 flat fee — [NerdWallet Balance Assist review](https://nerdwallet.com/personal-loans/reviews/bank-of-america-small-dollar-loan). One search result reported that effective August 14, 2026 the fee rises to $15, the range becomes $300-$500, and the term extends to four months; the specific source page was not identified among results (candidates: [rateschaser.com](https://rateschaser.com/personal-loans/reviews/bank-of-america-personal-loan/), [firstcard.app](https://www.firstcard.app/learn/bank-of-america-personal-loan)) and it was not confirmed on a Bank of America page — treat as unverified. Either way, the maximum is $500 (below $1,000).
- Wells Fargo Flex Loan: $250 or $500 for a flat $12 or $20 fee, four monthly installments, offered only to preapproved customers — [NerdWallet Flex Loan review](https://www.nerdwallet.com/personal-loans/reviews/wells-fargo-flex-small-personal-loan); [Credit Karma](https://www.creditkarma.com/news/i/wells-fargo-small-dollar-loans). Maximum $500.
- Regions Protection Line of Credit: $50 to $500, fixed rate, no annual or transfer fee; limit set by deposit activity, balances, and overdraft history and cannot be raised on request — [Regions Protection Line of Credit](https://regions.com/personal-banking/lines-of-credit/protection-line-of-credit); [Regions FAQ](https://regions.com/help/products-services/personal-credit-line/protection-line-of-credit/how-does-the-regions-protection-line-of-credit-work). Regions also lists a "Regions Credit Line" of $500 up to $3,000 — [Regions help](https://regions.com/help/online-exclusive-offers/credit-line/what-is-the-regions-credit-line)
- Huntington Standby Cash: launched 2021 as a $100-$1,000 line — [Huntington press release](https://ir.huntington.com/news-presentations/press-releases/detail/85/huntington-launches-standby-cashsm-to-give-eligible); Huntington's current consumer page cites $100-$750 — [Huntington](https://huntington.com/learn/budgeting/cash-before-paycheck). Eligibility: $1,000+ in monthly deposits to a Huntington personal checking account for three consecutive months and average daily balance of $200+; approval based mainly on checking/deposit history rather than credit score — [Huntington](https://www.huntington.com/learn/credit-debt/standby-cash-credit-score). Launch terms were no interest or fees with autopay; other Huntington pages mention a 5% cash advance fee (sources conflict on current cost).
- Truist: a 2023 AARP comparison lists "Truist Cash Reserve" with a $750 maximum and 18% APR — [AARP Bulletin Sept 2023](https://www.aarp.org/publications/bulletins/aarp-bulletin-september-2023/page7/) (older source)

### Inferences
- With roughly $5,300-$6,000/month gross income, the borrower clears the CLC Houston rule (loan up to half of gross monthly income, max $1,000) and Huntington's deposit thresholds; income is not the binding constraint — credit score, account/membership tenure, and employer participation are.
- "About three months at that income" is a tight but workable fit: CLC requires 3+ months with the employer; Capital Good Fund asks for two months of bank history; Huntington Standby Cash needs three consecutive months of deposits.
- Large-bank products do not help someone who is not already a customer with deposit history, and only U.S. Bank's reaches $1,000. Huntington has no Texas retail branch footprint as far as general knowledge goes, and no source retrieved here addressed that — verify before listing it as a Texas option.
- Oportun is a meaningfully costlier fallback (near 36% APR plus possible origination fee) than CLC (18% + $20) or Capital Good Fund, but still far below Texas payday/CAB pricing and does not require an employer or membership.

### Gaps
- Oportun's CDFI certification was not verified in this research (no source retrieved). Its current Texas-specific APR/fee disclosure was not read.
- No reliable source found for Security Service FCU, TDECU, A+ FCU, University FCU or Amplify lending $1,000+ unsecured to sub-580 members; minimum scores are generally unpublished. Share-secured/credit-builder loans at these institutions were not researched in detail.
- LiftFund: no results retrieved; it is known as a small-business microlender and no consumer personal loan was found. PeopleFund and BCL of Texas (outside the CLC program) appear to be business/housing lenders, not personal lenders — based on absence of a consumer product in results, not a direct confirmation.
- Mission Asset Fund: no source named a current Texas Lending Circles partner. Lending circles also deliver funds on a rotation, so they are not a fast $1,000 source.
- Truist's current small-dollar product name, limit, and Texas availability in 2026 not confirmed; the only data point is from 2023.
- Minimum job-tenure rules at Capital Good Fund and Oportun were not found beyond "have an income."

## 3. Is the Community Loan Center of Texas program available, what does it cost, and does it require the employer to participate?

### Takeaway
Yes, the CLC program exists across multiple Texas markets as a franchise network (Texas Community Capital is the network sponsor; local nonprofit lenders run each center), costs 18% interest plus a $20 fee on up to $1,000 over up to 12 months, and is available only to employees of an enrolled employer, repaid by payroll deduction. Most term sources are several years old; no 2026-dated rate sheet was retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Loan terms: maximum $1,000, 18% interest, $20 fee, payable over one year — [Texas Appleseed, "Fair Loans and Family Finances" (2023)](https://texasappleseed.org/sites/default/files/2023-05/fair-loans-and-family-finances.pdf); [Texas Appleseed news release](https://texasappleseed.org/new-report-affirms-access-fair-loans-improves-financial-well-being-texans)
- An average $941 CLC loan costs $1,056 to repay versus about $4,300 for a comparable payday loan — [Texas Appleseed](https://texasappleseed.org/new-report-affirms-access-fair-loans-improves-financial-well-being-texans)
- Up to 12 months to repay, no prepayment penalty, payments deducted from paychecks — [NeighborWorks America (2016)](https://www.neighborworks.org/Media-Center/Stories/2016/Community-Loan-Center-Small-dollar-loan-program) (older)
- Employer participation is required: loans are available "to employees of enrolled employers"; BCL of Texas operates the CLC of Dallas and CLC of Austin — [BCL of Texas Loan Centers](https://bcloftexas.org/community/loan-centers); CLC of Austin site — [clcofaustin.org](http://www.clcofaustin.org/home)
- Program is free to participating employers; employees apply online — [Texas Community Capital flyer (2015)](https://assetfunders.org/wp-content/uploads/NTX_Building_Financial_Capability_2015_CommunityLoanCenter.pdf) (older)
- Employer onboarding (Houston): sign a Memorandum of Understanding and schedule a few hours of payroll-staff training; CLC of Greater Houston was founded in 2014 and is licensed by the Texas Office of Consumer Credit Commission — search excerpt attributed to the Houston center's site (page URL surfaced as a Squarespace mirror: [what-we-do](https://paul-charles-tnqy.squarespace.com/what-we-do)); verify on the center's own domain
- City of Houston employee FAQ: eligible if a full-time employee, at least 18, employed over three months; loan up to half of gross monthly income not to exceed $1,000; one-year loans at 18% with a $20 administrative fee; payroll deduction — [City of Houston CLC FAQ](https://www.houstontx.gov/hr/hrfiles/benefits/FAQ-%20CLC.pdf) (undated)
- Brazos Valley: up to $1,000 at 18%, no credit history or collateral needed, 3+ months at a participating employer — [CLC of the Brazos Valley](https://clcbv.org/)
- Rio Grande Valley: minimum age 18, three months' employment with a participating employer — [RGV Community Loan Center](http://www.rgvcommunityloancenter.com/)
- An ICBA Independent Banker article describes CLC lenders typically charging a $20 origination fee and 18% interest — [ICBA Independent Banker](https://www.icba.org/w/small-dollar-loans-an-alternative-to-payday-lenders). A search summary dated this article August 2026, but the date was not verified.

### Inferences
- The CLC maximum equals, and does not exceed, the $1,000 target; a borrower needing more than $1,000 would need a second source.
- At 18% plus $20 over 12 months, total finance cost on $1,000 is roughly $120 (consistent with Texas Appleseed's $941 → $1,056 example), making it the cheapest non-PAL option found — but only if the employer is enrolled. If the employer is not enrolled, the borrower can ask HR to enroll (no cost to the employer), which is not a fast path.
- The borrower's possible "only about three months" of job history is right at the CLC tenure minimum.

### Gaps
- No current (2026) directory of CLC locations or participating employers was retrieved, and no 2026 confirmation that pricing is still 18% + $20 or that the cap is still $1,000. Texas Community Capital's own site (tccapital.org) was not retrieved.
- Which CLC markets are active in 2026 (Austin, Dallas, Houston, Brazos Valley, Rio Grande Valley were found; others such as San Antonio, Laredo, Waco, Lubbock were not checked) is unconfirmed.

## 4. Does Capital Good Fund or any similar nonprofit lend in Texas in 2026, and at what APR?

### Takeaway
Capital Good Fund lists Texas as a served state with no minimum FICO and no fees, but its own pages show conflicting Texas APRs (12% on one page, 18% on another, "varies by county" on a third) and conflicting maximums ($1,500 on its Impact Loan page vs. "up to $2,500" per United Way Dallas), so the exact 2026 Texas price must be confirmed at application. Third parties uniformly put it well under 36%.

### Cited Findings
- Capital Good Fund Impact Loan is available in Rhode Island, Florida, Texas, Delaware, Illinois, New Jersey, and Connecticut — [Capital Good Fund Impact Loan overview](https://capitalgoodfund.org/impact-loan-overview/)
- Texas APR as shown on Capital Good Fund pages: one page shows "Texas · 12% with ZERO closing fees"; another shows "$300 to $1,500 • 18% Annual Percentage Rate"; the Texas page says APR depends on county of residence and the final APR appears at application — [Impact Loan overview](https://capitalgoodfund.org/impact-loan-overview/); [Texas page](https://capitalgoodfund.org/overview/impact-loans-tx-2/); [Spanish Texas page](https://capitalgoodfund.org/es/overview/impact-loans-tx-2/). These conflict; pages are undated.
- NerdWallet lists estimated APR 12.00%-15.99%, says rates are capped at 18%, lists a lender-wide range of $300 to $50,000, and notes the Impact loan maximum of $1,500 — [NerdWallet Capital Good Fund review](https://www.nerdwallet.com/personal-loans/reviews/capital-good-fund-personal-loans)
- Bankrate lists an APR range of 0%-12% across Capital Good Fund's product lineup — [Bankrate](https://bankrate.com/loans/personal-loans/reviews/capital-good-funds)
- United Way of Metropolitan Dallas (Capital Good Fund partner) describes small-dollar loans up to $2,500 at APR not exceeding 35%, and conversion loans up to $4,000 — [United Way Dallas](https://unitedwaydallas.org/resources/affordable-loans/). The 35% is a ceiling statement, not the quoted rate; it conflicts in framing with the 12%-18% figures above.
- Capital Good Fund entered Texas in late 2020 — [KERA News (Dec. 2020)](https://www.keranews.org/business-economy/2020-12-02/new-nonprofit-fund-offers-alternatives-to-predatory-loan-industry-in-texas) (older); expansion funded by a $700K JPMorgan Chase commitment — [Dallas Innovates](https://dallasinnovates.com/?p=167074) (older)
- Requirements and terms: bank account or approved prepaid card, income, two months of bank history through Plaid, government ID; no minimum FICO; zero application/closing costs; 15-month term — [Impact Loan overview](https://capitalgoodfund.org/impact-loan-overview/)
- Other nonprofit options in Texas: Community Loan Centers at 18% + $20 (section 3); Mission Asset Fund lending circles at 0% through partner nonprofits — [Wikipedia: MAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Asset_Fund)

### Inferences
- A $1,000 request fits within every stated Capital Good Fund Impact Loan cap ($1,500 or $2,500), so the cap conflict does not affect the minimum need; it matters only if the borrower wants more than $1,500.
- Capital Good Fund is the only nonprofit found that lends $1,000+ statewide (or in served counties) in Texas without an employer tie, membership tenure, or minimum score, making it the leading lower-cost candidate for this profile.
- "APR depends on county" suggests Texas pricing or availability may be tied to locally funded loan-loss reserves (e.g., the United Way Dallas initiative); availability outside funded counties should be confirmed.

### Gaps
- Capital Good Fund's site could not be fetched (capitalgoodfund.org is outside the session's allowed network domains), so the current Texas APR, maximum amount, and county coverage as of November 2026 are unconfirmed; no 2026-dated primary statement was found.
- Whether Capital Good Fund has a minimum time-on-job requirement, and whether past-due/charged-off accounts or open payday loans disqualify, was not found.

## 5. How quickly can a new member get funded at each?

### Takeaway
Funding-speed data was the weakest area: almost no source retrieved gave a verified time-to-fund. What can be said is structural — products with membership/account-tenure rules are not available to a brand-new member at all for 1-12 months, while Capital Good Fund, Oportun, and CLC (if the employer is enrolled) have no tenure wait beyond bank/job history.

### Cited Findings
- PAL I requires at least one month of membership; PAL II has no federal membership waiting period — [Gerald summary](https://joingerald.com/learn/cash-advance/payday-alternatives-disclosure-rules); [MyCreditUnion.gov](https://mycreditunion.gov/manage-your-money/consumer-loans-credit-cards/payday-alternative-loans)
- Credit Human QMoney: deposit account must be open 6+ months — [Credit Human](https://credithuman.com/personal/personal-loans/qmoney-loan/)
- Texas Plains FCU: 10 months' membership required; "instant loan decision" once eligible — [Texas Plains FCU](https://www.texasplainsfederal.org/loans/unsecured-loans/)
- Neighborhood CU Quick Cash: 12 consecutive months of membership — [Neighborhood CU](https://myncu.com/borrow/quick-cash-loan)
- CLC: employee must have 3+ months with a participating employer; application is online — [CLC of the Brazos Valley](https://clcbv.org/); [Texas Community Capital flyer (2015)](https://assetfunders.org/wp-content/uploads/NTX_Building_Financial_Capability_2015_CommunityLoanCenter.pdf)
- Capital Good Fund: requires two months of banking history via Plaid — [Impact Loan overview](https://capitalgoodfund.org/impact-loan-overview/)
- Huntington Standby Cash: three consecutive months of $1,000+ deposits required; described as an instant line once eligible — [Huntington](https://www.huntington.com/learn/credit-debt/standby-cash-credit-score); [Huntington press release](https://ir.huntington.com/news-presentations/press-releases/detail/85/huntington-launches-standby-cashsm-to-give-eligible)
- Wells Fargo Flex Loan is offered only to preapproved existing customers — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/personal-loans/reviews/wells-fargo-flex-small-personal-loan)

### Inferences
- Rough ordering for a brand-new applicant needing $1,000 (by eligibility wait, not verified processing time): Oportun and Capital Good Fund (no membership wait); CLC (no wait if employer enrolled and 3 months on the job); Navy Federal or RBFCU signature loan (can join and apply together, but approval under 580 is uncertain); U.S. Bank Simple Loan and other bank products (existing customers only); tenure-gated PAL-type loans (6-12 months).
- Because the cheapest products are tenure-gated, joining a federal credit union now has value mainly for future borrowing.
