# Online Subprime Personal Installment Lenders in Texas (FICO under 580, loan of $1,000+), as of November 2026

**Method caveat (read first):** Direct page fetches were blocked in this research session (lender sites, LendingTree, etc. were outside the session's allowed network domains). Every finding below comes from web-search result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the full page. Lender-owned pages (opploans.com, risecredit.com, oportun.com, possiblefinance.com, upstart.com help pages, onemainfinancial.com) were surfaced in search and are cited where the search summary quoted them, but none could be opened and read end-to-end. Review-site figures conflict with each other in several places; conflicts are flagged, not resolved. The borrower should treat every APR below as a range to be confirmed by a soft-pull prequalification. Payday and auto title loans are excluded.

## Which of these lenders actually serve Texas and accept scores under 580 or have no minimum score?

### Takeaway
Lenders with evidence of Texas service and no stated minimum score: Upstart, OneMain Financial, Oportun, OppLoans, Rise Credit, Integra Credit, Possible Finance (but capped at $750 in Texas, so it fails the $1,000 need), and probably NetCredit and CreditNinja (Texas availability not confirmed from a primary source). Avant (550-580), Universal Credit (about 560), and Upgrade (560-620 depending on source) sit right at the borrower's score line; LendingPoint (585-600+) is effectively out.

### Inferences
- Possible Finance cannot meet the $1,000 minimum in Texas (cap $750) and should be dropped from the shortlist unless the borrower accepts less.
- Texas's usual consumer-loan rate limits do not constrain OppLoans/Rise/NetCredit pricing because those loans are originated by out-of-state partner banks (the OppLoans search summary states this explicitly); this is why triple-digit APRs are legal online in Texas.
- Practical tiers for a sub-580 Texas borrower: Tier A (36% or less, no score floor): Upstart, OneMain, Oportun. Tier B (36% or less, floor near 550-580, borderline): Avant, Universal Credit, Upgrade. Tier C (about 99%-199%+, no floor): OppLoans, Rise, NetCredit (lower band), CreditNinja, Integra.

## Which base approval mainly on income and bank account cash flow rather than credit score?

### Takeaway
OppLoans, Possible Finance, Oportun, NetCredit and Rise underwrite primarily on income and bank activity; Upstart uses an alternative model (education, employment, income) on top of credit; OneMain looks at income versus expenses and can take collateral.

### Cited Findings
- OppLoans: no score required and no hard pull; approval relies mainly on income and bank activity. — [Finder best bad credit loans](https://www.finder.com/personal-loans/best-bad-credit-personal-loans)
- OppLoans eligibility: official FAQ names direct deposit as a requirement; third-party sources add a checking or savings account with about 90 days of history (one says 60), regular income from employment or benefits, and some cite a $1,500 minimum monthly income. — [OppLoans eligibility FAQ](https://opploans.com/faqs/what-are-the-requirements-and-eligibility-to-apply-for-a-loan); [Finder OppLoans review](https://www.finder.com/short-term-loans/opploans-loans); [MyBankTracker OppLoans 2026](https://www.mybanktracker.com/personal-loans/reviews/opploans-personal-loans-308075)
- Possible Finance: evaluates income, cash flow and transaction history from the linked bank account rather than FICO; reports to TransUnion and Experian. — [Possible Finance Texas page](https://www.possiblefinance.com/states/texas); [MyBankTracker Possible review](https://www.mybanktracker.com/personal-loans/reviews/possible-finance-personal-loans-308273)
- Oportun: evaluates ability to repay based on earnings versus housing costs and other expenses; no credit score required; requires government ID, SSN or ITIN, proof of residency and proof of income; no fixed minimum income per LendingTree (one SuperMoney comparison page cites $12,000 a year). Soft-pull prequalification. Reports payments to credit bureaus. — [LendingTree Oportun review](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/reviews/oportun/); [NerdWallet Oportun review](https://www.nerdwallet.com/personal-loans/reviews/oportun-personal-loans); [SuperMoney Oportun vs Upstart](https://www.supermoney.com/oportun-vs-upstart)

- Rise: Texas eligibility is age 18+, Texas residency, a consistent source of income, an active checking account and a valid SSN; reports to TransUnion and Experian. — [Rise Dallas page](https://www.risecredit.com/loans/dallas); [Rise San Antonio page](https://www.risecredit.com/loans/san-antonio)
- Upstart: weighs education, employment and income alongside credit; minimum income is listed as none by NerdWallet, $12,000 a year via Credible's channel. — [NerdWallet Upstart 2026](https://www.nerdwallet.com/blog/loans/upstart-review-personal-loans-beginners); [Credible Upstart](https://www.credible.com/personal-loan/upstart-personal-loans-review)

### Inferences
- At roughly $5,300-$6,000 gross per month, the borrower clears every published or rumored income minimum found ($1,200/month Avant, $1,500/month OppLoans, $12,000/year Upstart-via-Credible and Oportun) by a wide margin. Income level is not the obstacle; score, short income history, and existing debt load are.
- Cash-flow underwriters look at the bank account itself, so recent overdrafts/NSFs and whether pay arrives by direct deposit matter more than the FICO number for OppLoans and similar lenders.

### Gaps
- Exact soft-pull versus hard-pull practice was only confirmed in search summaries for OppLoans (no hard pull) and Oportun (soft-pull prequalification). Upstart, OneMain, Avant, Upgrade/Universal Credit, NetCredit and Rise are widely known to offer soft-pull rate checks, but no source captured in this session states it; treat as unverified here.
- Bureau reporting was confirmed in summaries only for Rise (TransUnion, Experian), Possible (TransUnion, Experian) and Oportun ("credit bureaus"). Not captured for OppLoans, NetCredit, CreditNinja, Integra, Upstart, OneMain, Avant in this session.

- Avant 9.95%-35.99% APR. — [LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/installment-loans-for-bad-credit/)

- Consumer advocates generally treat 36% APR as the ceiling for an affordable loan. — [NerdWallet OppLoans review](https://www.nerdwallet.com/personal-loans/reviews/opploans-personal-loans)

### Inferences
These are my own amortization estimates (equal monthly payments, no fees, $1,500 principal), not lender quotes. Actual schedules are often biweekly and fees change the figures.
- 35.99% APR, 24 months: about $89/month, about $2,125 repaid, about $625 interest. With a 10% origination/administrative fee the borrower nets about $1,350 of the $1,500, or must borrow about $1,667 to net $1,500.
- 99% APR, 12 months: about $202/month, about $2,420 repaid, about $920 interest.
- 160% APR, 12 months: about $257/month, about $3,090 repaid, about $1,590 interest.
- 199% APR, 12 months: about $296/month, about $3,550 repaid, about $2,050 interest.
- A sub-580 borrower who is approved by Upstart, OneMain, Oportun or Avant should expect the top of the range (about 30%-36% plus a fee), and at OppLoans/Rise should expect the upper half (NerdWallet/LendingTree's 160%-195% band for OppLoans suggests typical pricing sits there). A triple-digit loan costs roughly 2.5 to 3.5 times as much interest as a 36% loan over a shorter period.
- All the triple-digit lenders reportedly have no prepayment penalty (confirmed in summaries for OppLoans; reported for Rise in most states), so early payoff sharply cuts cost.

### Gaps
- No lender-published Texas payment example for a $1,000-$2,000 loan was captured for any lender.
- Texas-specific origination/administrative fee amounts for OneMain, Oportun and Upstart were not found.
- NetCredit, CreditNinja and Integra Texas-specific APRs were not confirmed.

## Do any require longer than three months of income or job history?

### Takeaway
No lender was found that publishes a job-tenure requirement longer than three months; the tightest reported rules are OppLoans' roughly 90-day bank account history and an older (2019) three-months-with-current-employer rule. The real friction for a three-month earner is 1099 income, which Upstart and OneMain typically verify with tax returns.

### Cited Findings
- OppLoans: a 2019 promotional page and one review say three months with the current employer; newer sources omit a tenure rule. Bank account age is most often cited as 90 days (one source says 60). Direct deposit is required per the official FAQ. — [OppLoans eligibility FAQ](https://opploans.com/faqs/what-are-the-requirements-and-eligibility-to-apply-for-a-loan); [Finder OppLoans](https://www.finder.com/short-term-loans/opploans-loans); [Hustler Money Blog 2019 (outdated)](https://www.hustlermoneyblog.com/tag/opploans-review-2019-50-referral-bonus-promotion/)
- Upstart (W-2): a recent pay stub (within 30 days), or an official job offer letter stating compensation and start date if newly employed; lenders may contact the employer. No months-of-history minimum was found. — [Upstart help: documents needed](https://upstarthelp.upstart.com/borrower-verification/documents-needed-to-apply-for-a-loan); [Upstart help: income verification documentation](https://upstarthelp.upstart.com/7621-verification/income-verification-documentation)
- Upstart (1099/self-employed): most recent full tax return (Form 1040) plus a bank statement showing recent deposits; independent contractors can provide a current contract with compensation details. Upstart's own guide says 1099 income counts when it holds up over time and lenders usually verify it with 1-2 years of tax returns. — [Upstart help: documents needed](https://upstarthelp.upstart.com/borrower-verification/documents-needed-to-apply-for-a-loan); [Upstart income-based loans guide](https://www.upstart.com/learn/income-based-loans/)
- OneMain: proof of income such as pay stubs or tax returns; self-employed applicants must provide tax returns; may ask for employer phone number and manager name. No tenure rule found. — [OneMain Waco branch page](https://www.onemainfinancial.com/branches/TX/Waco/76710/4196); [LendingTree OneMain review](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/reviews/onemain-financial)
- Oportun: requires proof of income; no tenure rule surfaced. — [NerdWallet Oportun review](https://www.nerdwallet.com/personal-loans/reviews/oportun-personal-loans)
- Rise: "consistent source of income" and an active checking account; no tenure rule surfaced. — [Rise Dallas page](https://www.risecredit.com/loans/dallas)

### Inferences
- If the borrower is W-2 with three months of pay stubs and direct deposit, the three-month history should satisfy every lender's documented requirements found here, including OppLoans' 90-day pattern (provided the bank account itself is at least about 90 days old).
- If the borrower is 1099, expect trouble at Upstart and OneMain (tax return showing this income may not exist yet); a signed contract plus three months of bank deposits is the fallback at Upstart. Bank-link cash-flow lenders (OppLoans, Rise, NetCredit, CreditNinja) are more likely to accept three months of observed deposits, though OppLoans' direct-deposit requirement may be an issue for contractor pay that is not payroll direct deposit.

### Gaps
- No current primary-source statement of minimum employment length was captured for any lender; OppLoans' three-month rule rests on a 2019 page and may be outdated.
- Document checklists for Oportun, NetCredit, CreditNinja, Integra, Avant and Universal Credit (pay stub count, bank-link provider, 1099 handling) were not retrieved.

## Which have the highest likelihood of approval for this profile, and which offer the lowest cost?

### Takeaway
Lowest cost if approved: Upstart, OneMain (secured or co-applicant option helps), Oportun and, borderline, Avant/Universal Credit, all capped near 36% APR. Highest approval odds: OppLoans and Rise (then NetCredit, CreditNinja, Integra) at roughly 99%-199%+ APR. A sensible order is soft-pull prequalify at the 36% lenders first, then NetCredit, and only then the triple-digit lenders.

### Cited Findings
- Upstart, OneMain and Oportun have no minimum score and cap near 36% APR. — [LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/installment-loans-for-bad-credit/); [NerdWallet OneMain](https://www.nerdwallet.com/reviews/loans/personal-loans/onemain-financial-personal-loans); [LendingTree Oportun](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/reviews/oportun/)
- LendingTree's Texas-specific 2026 ranking for bad credit: Upstart, OneMain Financial, Prosper, Raiz Federal Credit Union. — [LendingTree Texas bad credit loans 2026](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/loans-for-bad-credit-texas/)
- Despite "no minimum," one Finder piece notes most Upstart approvals are 580+. — [Finder best bad credit loans](https://www.finder.com/personal-loans/best-bad-credit-personal-loans)
- OppLoans is described as a last resort due to cost, with approval based on income and bank activity rather than score. — [Finder best bad credit loans](https://www.finder.com/personal-loans/best-bad-credit-personal-loans)
- Texas credit unions are cited as cheaper bad-credit alternatives (Raiz FCU in LendingTree's list; TDECU in Elite Personal Finance's Aug 2026 Texas guide). — [LendingTree Texas](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/loans-for-bad-credit-texas/); [Elite Personal Finance Texas bad credit loans, Aug 2026](https://www.elitepersonalfinance.com/best-bad-credit-loans-in-texas/)

### Inferences
- Suggested ranking by cost (lowest first), with my judgment of approval odds for FICO under 580, about $5,500/month, three months of history:
  1. Oportun: 36% cap, no score or credit file needed, income-and-expense underwriting, $300-$7,500 in Texas. Probably the best blend of cost and approval odds.
  2. OneMain Financial: 36% cap, no score floor, Texas branches, minimum $1,500; a vehicle as collateral or a co-applicant raises odds. Moderate odds.
  3. Upstart: 36% cap, minimum $1,000; no formal floor but approvals skew 580+. Low-to-moderate odds; W-2 better than 1099.
  4. Avant / Universal Credit / Upgrade: 36% cap; floors of about 550-580 make these a coin flip at best for a score in the 550-579 range and unlikely below 550.
  5. NetCredit: 34%-99.99%; no floor; $1,000 minimum. Middle cost, good odds if it serves Texas.
  6. OppLoans and Rise: 99%-199%; highest approval odds; $1,000+ available. Expensive.
  7. CreditNinja and Integra: similar or higher cost (Integra up to 399%); use only after the above.
  - Excluded: Possible Finance (Texas cap $750), LendingPoint (score and income floors too high).
- The borrower's income (about $64,000-$72,000 annualized) is strong relative to this segment; if existing debt payments are modest, approval at a 36% lender is plausible even under 580, so those soft-pull checks are worth doing before accepting a triple-digit offer.
