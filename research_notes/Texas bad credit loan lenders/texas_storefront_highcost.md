## 1. What are the legal rate and fee caps on Chapter 342 installment loans in Texas for a loan of about $1,000-$2,000, and what APR does that produce in practice?

### Takeaway
Texas has two regulated-lender tracks: Subchapter F "small installment" loans (cash advance up to $1,840 for July 1, 2026 - June 30, 2027) that the OCCC says run about 80%-113% APR, and Subchapter E loans capped at 30%/24%/18% tiered interest plus an administrative fee of up to $130, which works out to roughly 35%-50% APR on small balances. A $1,000-$1,840 loan from a traditional "small loan" storefront (World, Security, Sun, Western Shamrock, Toledo) will almost always be a Subchapter F loan at roughly 85%-110% APR; larger lenders (OneMain, Mariner, Regional, Lendmark) use Subchapter E and cap at about 36% APR but have higher minimum loan sizes.

### Cited Findings
- Subchapter F ceilings for loans made July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027: the ceiling amounts in Tex. Fin. Code §§342.251 and 342.259 are $920.00 and $1,840.00 respectively (prior year: $1,800; year before: $1,760) — [OCCC Texas Credit Letter, 2026](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Texas_Credit_Letters_2026.pdf); prior-year maxima from [OCCC 2025 consumer loan products study](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025_study_consumer_loan_products.pdf) and [OCCC 2024 study](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2024_study_consumer_loan_products_0.pdf)
- OCCC's own characterization of 342-F loans: approximate APR range 80%-113%; maximum loan $1,800 in the 2025 report (ceiling re-indexed each July 1) — [OCCC 2025 study](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025_study_consumer_loan_products.pdf)
- Subchapter F charge structure: an acquisition charge plus an "installment account handling charge" of not more than $4 a month for each $100 of cash advance (for advances over $100); small advances carry an acquisition charge of up to one-tenth of the cash advance — [Tex. Fin. Code §342.252 via public.law](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._fin._code_section_342.252); §342.259 (larger advances) likewise caps the handling charge at $4/month per $100 — [Tex. Fin. Code §342.259](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._fin._code_section_342.259)
- The acquisition charge is "not interest," is earned when the loan is made and is non-refundable; the installment account handling charge is refundable on prepayment under Subchapter H — [Tex. Fin. Code §342.252](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._fin._code_section_342.252); [§342.259](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._fin._code_section_342.259)
- Maximum Subchapter F loan term: for loans over $100, one month for each multiple of $20 of cash advance (so term limits are not binding on a $1,000+ loan; lenders in practice use 4-21 months) — [Tex. Fin. Code §342.255 via FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/finance-code/fin-sect-342-255/)
- A 2013 bill analysis (SB 1251) described the then-current Subchapter F acquisition charge as capped at $10 for loans over $100 and proposed raising it — [Senate Research Center analysis, C.S.S.B. 1251](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/83R/analysis/pdf/SB01251S.pdf). (Older; see Gaps on the current acquisition-charge cap.)
- Subchapter E (Tex. Fin. Code §342.201(e)) tiered rate brackets for July 1, 2026 - June 30, 2027: bracket amounts $4,600, $9,660 and $23,000; statutory tiers are 30% a year on the first bracket, 24% on the next, 18% on the top — [OCCC Texas Credit Letter 2026](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Texas_Credit_Letters_2026-1.pdf); tier percentages from [Tex. Fin. Code §342.201](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._fin._code_section_342.201)
- §342.201(a) add-on brackets for the same period: $2,760 and $23,000 — [OCCC Texas Credit Letter 2026](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Texas_Credit_Letters_2026-1.pdf)
- Subchapter E administrative fee under §342.201(g): $130 for 2026-27 (one notice says it was changed from $125, a Texas Register notice says it "remains" $130 — amount consistent, history unclear) — [OCCC Texas Credit Letter 2026-2](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Texas_Credit_Letters_2026-2.pdf); [Texas Register, Feb. 13, 2026](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/February132026/In%20Addition/In%20Addition.html)
- Secondary-source summary of the OCCC 2025 comparison of a $1,500 loan: 342-F about 92% APR; 342-E about 44% APR; CAB payday 365%-496% APR (not verified against the OCCC PDF) — [flashcashadvance.com summary](https://flashcashadvance.com/cash-advance/texas/)
- Secondary-source example at the Subchapter E cap: $3,000 over 24 months at 30% is about $167.74/month plus a fee of up to $130 — [LendUp Texas page](https://www.lendup.com/states/texas/)
- Lender-published Texas terms consistent with the caps: Sun Loan Texas — up to $1,700, APR 83.01%-119.12%, 4-18 months — [Sun Loan](https://www.sunloan.com/personal-installment-loans/); Western Shamrock Texas — up to $1,840 (statutory max) — [Western Shamrock Texas](https://westernshamrock.com/locations/texas)

### Inferences
- (Researcher arithmetic, assuming the current Subchapter F formula is an acquisition charge of the lesser of 10% of the cash advance or $100 plus $4 per $100 per month — the acquisition-charge cap is from my prior knowledge of the post-2013 statute and was NOT confirmed in this session; the handling charge is cited above.) $1,000 loan: 6 months = $340 charges, $223.33/mo, ~109% APR; 9 months = $460 charges, $162.22/mo, ~100% APR; 12 months = $580 charges, $131.67/mo, ~94% APR; 18 months = $820 charges, $101.11/mo, ~87% APR. $1,500 for 12 months = $820 charges, $193.33/mo, ~89% APR. $1,840 for 12 months = ~$983 charges, $235.27/mo, ~88% APR. These land inside the OCCC's stated 80%-113% range and near the ~92% figure quoted for a $1,500 loan, which supports the assumed formula.
- (Researcher arithmetic) Subchapter E at the 30% cap with the $130 administrative fee treated as a prepaid finance charge: $1,500 over 12 months = $146.23/mo, ~$255 interest + $130 fee, ~48% APR; $2,000 over 18 months ~40% APR; $3,000 over 24 months ~35% APR. This is why large Subchapter E lenders advertise "up to 35.99%" and why they set minimum loans of $1,500-$2,500+: the fee makes smaller loans exceed 36% APR.
- Practical consequence for a sub-580 borrower wanting just over $1,000: the product actually on offer at most Texas storefronts is a Subchapter F loan near 90%-110% APR, costing roughly $460-$580 in charges on $1,000 over 9-12 months. Paying early saves the unearned handling charge but not the acquisition charge.
- Credit insurance and other add-ons (commonly sold by traditional installment lenders) are outside these caps and can raise the effective cost; no session source quantified this for Texas (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Could not open the OCCC consumer-lending rate chart or the full text of §342.252/§342.259 to confirm the current acquisition-charge cap (10% of advance, max $100) — fetches were blocked; the only cited source on the acquisition charge is a 2013 bill analysis describing the pre-amendment $10 cap. Verify at statutes.capitol.texas.gov or the OCCC "Regulated Lenders" rate chart.
- The OCCC 2025 study's own $1,500 (or $1,000) side-by-side cost table could not be read directly; the 92%/44%/365-496% figures are from a secondary site.
- No Texas-specific data found on the cost of credit insurance/ancillary products on 342-F loans, or on late-fee/default-charge amounts.
- Subchapter G (second-lien) and Chapter 342 "signature loan" volume/average size data were not retrieved.

## 2. Which storefront installment lenders approve sub-580 borrowers with about $5,300-$6,000 monthly job income, and what documents and job history do they require?

### Takeaway
None of the Texas storefront installment lenders reviewed publishes a minimum credit score; all say they underwrite on credit history, income remaining after expenses, stability, and (for bigger loans) collateral, and several explicitly market to bad credit. Standard documents are a government photo ID, proof of income (paystub), and proof of residence; no lender publishes a minimum job-tenure requirement, and the small-loan companies (Sun Loan, Western Shamrock, Security Finance, World) advertise same-day or 30-minute decisions.

### Cited Findings
**Small-loan (Subchapter F-type) storefronts — loans roughly $450-$1,840 in Texas**
- Sun Loan (San Antonio-based): Texas loans up to $1,700, APR 83.01%-119.12%, terms 4-18 months; loans of $1,750-$10,000 require adequate collateral; approval depends on credit history, income after expenses, and collateral — [Sun Loan installment loans](https://www.sunloan.com/personal-installment-loans/)
- Sun Loan Texas requirements: government ID showing Texas residence, proof of income, age 18+; self-employed can use bank statements or tax returns; soft inquiry to prequalify, hard inquiry if the offer is accepted; approved applicants "can usually get their money the same day" — [Sun Loan Texas online loans](https://www.sunloan.com/online-loans/texas/)
- Sun Loan may ask for personal references (family/friends) — third-party review, not the lender's page — [Finanso Sun Loan Texas](https://finanso.com/us/loans/sunloan/texas/)
- Western Shamrock / Western Finance: Texas installment loans up to $1,840 (amount set by statute; its application page still showed $1,760 and a third-party review $1,700 — stale pages) — [Western Shamrock Texas](https://westernshamrock.com/locations/texas); [Western Shamrock get-started](https://westernshamrock.com/get-started)
- Western Shamrock documents: valid government-issued ID, proof of residence (signed lease or utility bill), proof of income (paystub or tax return), more may be requested — [Western Shamrock FAQ](https://westernshamrock.com/faq); [Western Shamrock installment loans](https://westernshamrock.com/installment-loans)
- Western Shamrock weighs debt-to-income ratio, credit history, and "employment and residency stability"; markets "personal loans for bad credit"; says it can approve within 30 minutes of receiving the application and documents and then send funds; cannot lend to military members or their dependents — [Western Shamrock bad credit loans](https://westernshamrock.com/loans/bad-credit-loans); [Western Shamrock installment loans](https://westernshamrock.com/installment-loans)
- Security Finance: Texas installment loan amounts $448 to $1,801; repayment periods 8-21 months; "highly qualified customers may be offered a maximum APR of 35.1%" (i.e., the published figure is the best case, not the typical Texas rate); subject to credit limitations and underwriting — [Security Finance about our loans](https://www.securityfinance.com/about-our-loans/)
- World Finance (World Acceptance): loans roughly $450-$10,000 (up to $12,000 in some states); larger amounts require a first lien on a motor vehicle; terms up to ~46 months; no set minimum credit score per review; considers credit history, income after monthly expenses, collateral; needs proof of income and government photo ID; age 18+ — [World Finance rates and terms](https://www.loansbyworld.com/rates-and-terms); [SuperMoney review](https://www.supermoney.com/reviews/personal-loans/world-finance-personal-loans); [Finanso review](https://finanso.com/us/loansbyworld/)
- World Finance says borrowers with bad credit may still qualify; a credit check is required (soft pull on application, hard pull on approval) — [World Finance FAQ](https://www.loansbyworld.com/faq-tags/personal-installment-loans)
- World Finance reports payment activity monthly to all three bureaus (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion) — [World Finance](https://worldfinanceusa.com/); [credit-building page](https://www.loansbyworld.com/loans/loans-for-building-credit)

**Larger (Subchapter E-type) branch lenders — capped near 36% APR but higher minimums**
- OneMain Financial: loans $1,500-$30,000; APR 11.99%-35.99% (verified Oct. 14, 2026 by the source); origination fee either flat $25-$500 or 1%-10% depending on state, financed into the loan; no minimum credit score to qualify — [FinanceRadar OneMain (Oct 2026)](https://financeradar.com/personal-loans/onemain-financial-personal-loan); [NerdWallet OneMain vs Mariner](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/loans/personal-loans/onemain-financial-mariner-finance-compare-personal-loans)
- Mariner Finance: loans $1,000-$25,000; APR 16.00%-35.99%; no stated minimum credit score; origination fee may apply by state; operates in 28 states (Texas presence not confirmed by the snippet) — [Mariner Finance](https://marinerfinanceapp.com/); [LendingTree Mariner review](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/reviews/mariner-finance/)
- Regional Finance (Regional Management): site lists $2,501-$45,000, APR 24.00%-35.99%, terms 24-60 months; larger amounts need a first lien on a qualifying vehicle; no minimum credit score stated; approval depends on "responsible credit history," sufficient income after monthly expenses, and collateral — [Regional Finance personal loan](https://regionalfinance.com/personal-loan/); [SuperMoney Regional review](https://www.supermoney.com/reviews/personal-loans/regional-finance-personal-loans)
- A third-party review reports some Regional Finance customers quoted rates of 60% or more depending on state (consistent with Regional also writing small Subchapter F-type loans in Texas, but not confirmed) — [Finder Regional Finance review](https://www.finder.com/personal-loans/regional-finance-review)

### Inferences
- With $5,300-$6,000/month in verifiable job income, income is not the binding constraint for a $1,000-$1,840 loan at any of these lenders (a 12-month Subchapter F payment on $1,000 is roughly $130/month, about 2%-2.5% of gross income); approval will turn on existing debt load, recent delinquencies/charge-offs, open bankruptcies, and stability of job and address.
- Best-fit routing for a sub-580 borrower: (1) try OneMain/Mariner/Regional first because a 36%-capped loan costs less than half of a Subchapter F loan, accepting that a sub-580 file may be declined or asked for a vehicle title as collateral or a co-applicant; (2) fall back to World, Security, Sun Loan, Western Shamrock/Western Finance, Toledo Finance at ~85%-110% APR, which exist specifically for this credit tier.
- Bring to the branch: driver's license/state ID, most recent paystubs (the three months of pay history the borrower has is more than these lenders ask for), a utility bill or lease, Social Security number, bank account details, and a list of personal references.
- Because these lenders pull credit and (at least World, and by industry norm OneMain/Mariner/Regional) report to the bureaus, on-time repayment can help rebuild a sub-580 score, whereas CAB payday/title loans typically do not build credit.

### Gaps
- No lender publishes a minimum length of employment; I found no reliable source for specific job-tenure cutoffs (e.g., "3 or 6 months on the job"). Branches set this case by case — call ahead.
- No usable results for Toledo Finance, Heights Finance, or Lendmark (Texas presence, amounts, APRs, reporting). Mariner's and Lendmark's Texas branch footprint was not confirmed.
- Credit-bureau reporting was confirmed only for World Finance. No explicit statement found for Sun Loan, Western Shamrock, Security Finance, Regional, or Toledo.
- OneMain's and Mariner's Texas-specific origination/administrative fee amount was not found (Texas law would cap it at the $130 §342.201(g) fee for a Subchapter E loan — inference).
- Actual approval rates for sub-580 applicants at any of these lenders are not published.
- Could not use the OCCC license lookup (ALECS) to verify current license status of any lender; a consumer should confirm at occc.texas.gov.

## 3. What are average APRs and fees for Texas payday and title loans per the latest OCCC data, and how much would a $1,000 loan cost in total?

### Takeaway
Texas places no cap on Credit Access Business fees; OCCC-derived averages put payday loans at roughly 415%-500% APR (about $34 per $100 per month) and title loans at roughly 200%-270% APR (about $19 per $100 per month), with typical terms of 4-5 months and heavy refinancing. On those averages a $1,000 payday loan costs on the order of $1,000-$1,400 in fees over four months and a $1,000 title loan about $1,000 over five months, plus the risk of losing the vehicle.

### Cited Findings
- Structure: CABs (Tex. Fin. Code Ch. 393) broker loans from third-party lenders; the lender's interest is 10% or less, the CAB fee is not capped, and "the annual percentage rate can routinely exceed 400 percent" — [OCCC 2025 consumer loan products study](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025_study_consumer_loan_products.pdf)
- A CAB "may assess fees for its services as agreed to between the parties" — [Tex. Fin. Code §393.222 context via FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/finance-code/fin-sect-393-222/)
- OCCC 2024 study (2024 Q2 fee data): non-amortizing installment title loans average about 272% APR; fully amortizing installment payday loans average about 488% APR; average terms about 4 months for payday and 5 months for title — [OCCC 2024 study](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2024_study_consumer_loan_products_0.pdf)
- OCCC-mandated CAB cost-disclosure form: average payday APR 415%, average auto title APR 223%; average fees per $100 borrowed over one month $34.14 (payday) and $19.45 (title). A secondary source says these disclosure figures derive from 2022 data (older — flag) — [Cash Central Texas CAB disclosure](https://www.cashcentral.com/Content/files/CAB_disclosure_for_Texas_combined.pdf); dating per [Madgett Law](https://www.madgettlaw.com/news/minnesota-vs-texas-payday-loan-rate-cap-47-60/)
- Advocacy summaries: payday loans average about 500% APR or more and auto title about 200% (title 200%-400% in another brief) — [Texas Fair Lending Alliance, The Basics](https://www.texasfairlending.org/the-basics); [RAISE Texas, Dec. 2024](https://raisetexas.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/RAISE-Texas-Small-Dollar-Loan-List.pdf); [TFLA brief 2023](https://www.texasappleseed.org/sites/default/files/2023-05/tfla-payday-and-auto-title-lending-brief-2023-0.pdf)
- Refinancing: 44% of Texas payday and auto title loan volume in 2024 was refinances; borrowers who refinance pay on average $1,200 to over $3,000 to repay a $500 loan — [Texas Fair Lending Alliance, The Basics](https://www.texasfairlending.org/the-basics)
- More than 30% of single-installment payday loans in 2024 were refinances (Texas Appleseed analysis of state data) — [NBC DFW](https://www.nbcdfw.com/investigations/squeezed-struggling-families-payday-lenders-often-unwilling-to-negotiate-missed-payments/3943295/?amp=1)
- Repossession: news reports citing Texas Appleseed say more than 25% (another piece: 27%) of Texas title-loan borrowers had their car repossessed in 2024 — [NBC DFW investigation](https://www.nbcdfw.com/investigations/squeezed-struggling-families-payday-lenders-often-unwilling-to-negotiate-missed-payments/3943295/?amp=1); [NBC 5 Responds](https://www.nbcdfw.com/news/nbc-5-responds/north-texas-consumers-warn-payday-and-title-loans-can-spiral-into-debt-trap/3943722/?amp=1); contrasted with the OCCC 2025 study, which says repossessions have leveled off and puts them at about 4.2% of active title loans (different denominator; snippet was fragmentary) — [OCCC 2025 study](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025_study_consumer_loan_products.pdf)
- Market size/trend: cumulative CAB fees about $22 billion from 2012 to 2024; 5.2% of Texas adults used a payday or title loan in 2024 (4.7% in 2020) — [Texas Fair Lending Alliance history](https://www.texasfairlending.org/history-1); [TFLA who borrows](https://www.texasfairlending.org/who-borrows-1)
- Loan counts fell in 2025: new auto title loans Q1-Q3 2025 were 141,051 versus 147,878 in the same period of 2024 — [Finance Commission of Texas packet, Feb. 20, 2026](https://www.fc.texas.gov/sites/default/files/2026-02/02202026-fc-packet.pdf)
- Local example (2023, Fort Worth-Arlington MSA): 59,891 new loans; about $78 million (38%) of $205 million in loan volume went to fees — [Rainwater Charitable Foundation](https://rainwatercharitablefoundation.org/short-term-loans-long-term-costs-payday-and-auto-title-lending-in-fort-worth/)
- The latest official datasets exist but were unreadable here: CY2025 CAB Annual Data Report (state summary as of March 24, 2026) and Q1 2026 quarterly — [OCCC 2025 Annual State](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025_Annual_State.pdf); [OCCC Q1 2026](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Q1_2026_State_Formatted.pdf)
- CABs must post a fee schedule conspicuously at each location (use it to get the exact cost for a given company) — [Tex. Fin. Code §393.222](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/finance-code/fin-sect-393-222/)

### Inferences
- (Researcher arithmetic from the disclosure-form averages; non-amortizing illustration) $1,000 payday loan at $34.14 per $100 per month for the 4-month average term = about $1,366 in fees, i.e., roughly $2,366 repaid. $1,000 title loan at $19.45 per $100 per month for 5 months = about $973 in fees, roughly $1,973 repaid. Amortizing installment structures would cost somewhat less in dollars at the same APR; refinancing costs more. Treat as order-of-magnitude: $1,000 borrowed from a CAB typically costs $1,000 or more in fees.
- The "more than 25% repossessed" (borrower-based, annual) and "4.2% of active title loans" (loan-based) statistics are not necessarily contradictory; they use different bases. The report should present both with that caveat rather than choose one.
- The 415%/223% disclosure-form figures are the oldest (2022-based); the 488%/272% OCCC figures (2024 Q2 data) are the most recent official APRs I could capture.

### Gaps
- CY2025 statewide CAB annual figures (average fee per $100 by product, loan counts, refinance counts, vehicles repossessed, locations) could not be extracted — the PDF was unreachable and search snippets were scrambled. This is the single most important item to verify directly.
- No company-specific Texas fee schedules or $1,000 APR examples were found for ACE Cash Express, Speedy Cash, Check 'n Go, Advance America, TitleMax, or CashNetUSA. Their Texas store/online status in 2026 was also not verified.
- The 2025 OCCC study's CAB APR estimates (the edition after the 272%/488% figures) were not captured, apart from the secondary-source 365%-496% payday range.

## 4. What city ordinances and state or federal protections apply, and what are the warning signs of loan scams (advance-fee loans) targeting bad-credit borrowers?

### Takeaway
About 46-49 Texas cities (including Dallas, Austin, San Antonio, Houston, El Paso) have adopted the "unified ordinance" capping payday loans at 20% of gross monthly income and title loans at the lesser of 3% of gross annual income or 70% of vehicle value, with a 4-installment / 3-rollover limit and 25% principal paydown per payment; state law itself requires only licensing, disclosures and fee posting, and the federal payment-withdrawal rule is in effect but de-prioritized by the CFPB. Any "lender" that guarantees approval or demands a fee before funding is a scam.

### Cited Findings
**City ordinances**
- Austin ordinance terms: payday loans cannot exceed 20% of the consumer's gross monthly income; auto title loans capped at the lesser of 3% of gross annual income or 70% of the vehicle's retail value; the city takes complaints — [City of Austin payday and auto lender complaints](https://www.austintexas.gov/page/payday-and-auto-lender-complaints)
- Austin, Dallas, El Paso and San Antonio ordinances: no more than 4 installments or 3 rollovers/renewals; each installment or renewal payment must reduce the original loan principal by 25% — [Texas Legislature handout, Payday and Auto Title City Ordinances (2013, older)](https://www.legis.state.tx.us/tlodocs/83R/handouts/C2702013021814001/eb602c4d-fb41-4040-94a9-4f777d5b0d1a.PDF); [TML Summary of Texas Local Ordinances](https://www.tml.org/DocumentCenter/View/384/Summary-of-Texas-Ordinances-PDF)
- First four adopting cities were Austin, Dallas, San Antonio and El Paso — [Loyola JSRI](https://jsri.loyno.edu/fourth-texas-city-passes-ordinance-limiting-payday-loans); Houston, San Antonio, Midland, Somerset, South Houston, Universal City, West University Place and others are in Texas Appleseed's ordinance list — [Texas Appleseed city ordinance summary (2015, older)](https://texasappleseed.org/sites/default/files/2023-05/city-ordinance-summary.pdf)
- Count of adopting cities: 49 since 2011 per Texas Fair Lending Alliance; 46 per a Goodwill East Texas report (2025) — [TFLA history](https://www.texasfairlending.org/history-1); [Goodwill East Texas report](https://goodwilleasttexas.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/report_on_impact_of_predatory_lending_on_lives_and_economy_in_tyler_texas.pdf)
- Dallas expanded its ordinance in 2021 (to reach other high-cost installment products); a 2025 Fifth Circuit ruling found the 2021 Dallas ordinance is not preempted by state law — [TFLA history](https://www.texasfairlending.org/history-1)
- Texas Appleseed research finds the unified ordinance leads to fewer negative outcomes (e.g., an estimated 7 fewer title-loan repossessions per 10,000 adults if applied statewide) — [Texas Appleseed, effect of unified ordinance](https://texasappleseed.org/sites/default/files/2023-05/effectofunifiedordinance.pdf)

**State protections**
- CABs must be licensed by the OCCC, give a written, dated, signed contract and pre- and post-loan cost disclosures (2012 rules), post fee schedules, and file quarterly/annual data reports — [Texas Legislature CAB update handout (2013, older)](https://www.legis.state.tx.us/tlodocs/83R/handouts/C2702013042914301/1b32f392-d511-466e-8dd4-61418a8da5e3.PDF); [7 TAC ch. 83 definitions](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/7-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-83-102)
- A CAB contract may not exceed 180 days (secondary source, not verified against statute) — [observed.org](https://observed.org/payday-loan-laws-in-texas/)
- No state cap on CAB fees or on the number of refinances outside ordinance cities — [OCCC 2025 study](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025_study_consumer_loan_products.pdf)
- Complaints: OCCC online complaint portal and consumer helpline 800-538-1579 (number from a 2021 OCCC overview — older) — [OCCC agency overview, March 2021](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/87R/handouts/C3952021030308001/f9698844-a792-4a60-845c-d2450ff7be6e.PDF)
- Chapter 342 lenders, by contrast, are rate-capped, examined by the OCCC, and must keep per-loan records for examination — [Texas Register proposed rule, March 6, 2026](https://www.sos.texas.gov/texreg/pdf/backview/0306/0306prop.pdf)

**Federal protections**
- CFPB payday rule payment provisions (operative March 30, 2025): a lender may not attempt to debit a borrower's account after two consecutive failed attempts without new authorization, and must give payment notices — [NCLC](https://library.nclc.org/article/rule-bounced-payday-and-high-cost-loan-payments-now-effect)
- On March 28, 2025 the CFPB said it would not prioritize enforcement or supervision of those provisions; its 2026 regulatory agenda plans a proposed rule reconsidering the remaining provisions; the rule was reported still in force and unchanged as of October 2026 — [National Law Review (enforcement)](https://natlawreview.com/article/cfpb-plans-limited-enforcement-payday-lending-rule); [National Law Review (2026 agenda)](https://natlawreview.com/article/cfpbs-2026-regulatory-agenda-tees-long-awaited-nprm-reconsider-payday-lending-rule); [fintechlaw.ai](https://fintechlaw.ai/blog/cfpb-payday-rule-non-prioritization-not-a-repeal)
- Military borrowers: Western Shamrock states it cannot extend loans to military members or dependents (reflecting the federal Military Lending Act rate limit) — [Western Shamrock installment loans](https://westernshamrock.com/installment-loans)

**Advance-fee loan scam warning signs**
- FTC: no legitimate lender will tell you that paying a fee guarantees a loan; advance-fee scams target people with bad credit or trouble getting loans; report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov — [FTC, What To Know About Advance-Fee Loans](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-advance-fee-loans)
- FTC (older guidance, 2013 archive): legitimate lenders never "guarantee" or say you are likely to get a loan before you apply, especially with bad credit, no credit or a bankruptcy; a lender not interested in your credit history is a warning sign — [FTC Advance-Fee Loans (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130218184735/http://www.consumer.ftc.gov/articles/0078-advance-fee-loans)
- Other red flags: upfront "processing" or "insurance" fees, unsolicited contact, pressure to act fast, demands for payment by gift card — [Mayo Employees FCU](https://mayocreditunion.org/post/protect_yourself_from_loan_scams.html); [Finder](https://www.finder.com/personal-loans/personal-loan-scams); [Experian](https://stg1.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/signs-of-a-personal-loan-scam/)

### Inferences
- Where the borrower lives matters: with ~$5,300-$6,000 gross monthly income, an ordinance city would cap a payday loan at about $1,060-$1,200 (20% of gross monthly income) and a title loan at about $1,900-$2,160 (3% of ~$63,600-$72,000 annualized income) or 70% of vehicle value if lower, and force payoff within 4 installments. Outside ordinance cities, and for online CABs that claim not to be subject to local rules, none of these limits apply.
- Legitimate Texas lenders do charge fees (acquisition charge, administrative fee), but these are financed into or deducted from the loan at closing — never wired, Zelle'd or paid by gift card beforehand. Verifying an OCCC license before applying is the practical scam screen.
- The federal payment rule's protection is real on paper but weakly enforced federally in 2025-26; borrowers should rely more on revoking ACH authorization with their bank and complaining to the OCCC.

## 5. How do the storefront installment lenders compare on cost to the CAB payday/title products?

### Takeaway
On a like-for-like small loan, rate-capped Chapter 342 lenders are several times cheaper: roughly 36%-48% APR (Subchapter E) or 80%-113% APR (Subchapter F) versus roughly 270%-500% APR for CAB title and payday loans. For $1,000, that is about $460-$580 in charges over 9-12 months at a Subchapter F storefront versus roughly $1,000-$1,400 in fees over 4-5 months at a CAB, with no vehicle at stake and with credit-bureau reporting at least at some installment lenders.

### Cited Findings
- OCCC: 342-F loans approx. 80%-113% APR; CAB transactions "lack regulatory caps" and "tend to carry the highest costs," with APRs that "can routinely exceed 400 percent" — [OCCC 2025 study](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025_study_consumer_loan_products.pdf)
- $1,500 loan comparison attributed to the OCCC 2025 study: 342-E about 44% APR; 342-F about 92% APR; CAB payday 365%-496% APR — [flashcashadvance.com summary (secondary)](https://flashcashadvance.com/cash-advance/texas/)
- OCCC 2024 study averages: installment payday (CAB) about 488% APR; installment title (CAB) about 272% APR — [OCCC 2024 study](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2024_study_consumer_loan_products_0.pdf)
- Subchapter E lenders' published caps: OneMain 11.99%-35.99%; Mariner 16.00%-35.99%; Regional 24.00%-35.99% — [FinanceRadar OneMain](https://financeradar.com/personal-loans/onemain-financial-personal-loan); [Mariner Finance](https://marinerfinanceapp.com/); [Regional Finance](https://regionalfinance.com/personal-loan/)
- Subchapter F lender published Texas APR: Sun Loan 83.01%-119.12% — [Sun Loan](https://www.sunloan.com/personal-installment-loans/)
- 44% of 2024 CAB loan volume was refinances; refinancing borrowers pay $1,200 to over $3,000 on a $500 loan — [Texas Fair Lending Alliance](https://www.texasfairlending.org/the-basics)
- Title-loan repossession risk: more than 25% of 2024 Texas title borrowers per Texas Appleseed as reported by [NBC DFW](https://www.nbcdfw.com/investigations/squeezed-struggling-families-payday-lenders-often-unwilling-to-negotiate-missed-payments/3943295/?amp=1); 4.2% of active title loans per [OCCC 2025 study](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025_study_consumer_loan_products.pdf)
- World Finance reports to all three credit bureaus (credit-building potential) — [World Finance](https://worldfinanceusa.com/)
- Texas Appleseed reports that access to fair (lower-cost) loans improves Texans' financial well-being — [Texas Appleseed](https://texasappleseed.org/new-report-affirms-access-fair-loans-improves-financial-well-being-texans)

### Inferences
- Cost ladder for about $1,000-$1,500 (cheapest to most expensive), combining cited caps with the researcher's arithmetic in sections 1 and 3:
  1. Subchapter E branch lender (OneMain/Mariner/Regional; min loan $1,000-$2,501): ~36%-48% APR; e.g., $1,500 over 12 months costs roughly $255 interest plus up to $130 fee.
  2. Subchapter F small-loan storefront (World, Security, Sun, Western Shamrock, Toledo; max $1,840): ~85%-110% APR; $1,000 over 12 months costs about $580; $1,500 over 12 months about $820.
  3. CAB title loan: ~220%-270% APR; $1,000 over 5 months about $970+ in fees, vehicle at risk.
  4. CAB payday (single or installment): ~415%-500% APR; $1,000 over 4 months about $1,370 in fees; frequent refinancing pushes it higher.
- A Subchapter F loan is expensive in absolute terms (near triple-digit APR) but is fully amortizing, rate-capped, refundable in part on early payoff, unsecured, and usually reported to bureaus; it is the clearly better "last resort" than any CAB product for this borrower. The borrower's income comfortably supports the ~$130-$235 monthly payment.
- Borrower should ask each lender for the Truth in Lending APR and total of payments on the exact amount, decline optional credit insurance, and compare against credit-union alternatives (covered by other researchers).
