# Income-Based and Cash-Flow-Based Borrowing Options Not Dependent on Credit Score (Texas, sub-580 score, $1,000+ need, as of November 2026)

> Research-method caveat for the report writer: direct page fetches were blocked in this session (nerdwallet.com and occc.texas.gov did not resolve), so every finding below comes from web-search result summaries of the linked pages, not from a full read of the page. Many of the available 2026 pages are affiliate or competitor-authored (Gerald/joingerald.com, MoneyLion, Chime blog, FirstCard). Where sources conflict, both figures are given. Treat every dollar figure as "verify in the app/agreement before relying on it." All arithmetic APR conversions are in the Inferences sections and are my own calculations.

## Which options can actually deliver $1,000 or more (in one advance or quickly combined) to someone earning about $5,300-$6,000/month by direct deposit with only about three months of history?

### Takeaway
No single cash advance app reliably gives a new user $1,000 at once; realistic first-cycle limits are $20-$250 per app, and the advertised $500-$1,000 ceilings are reached only after months of deposit history and often only after moving direct deposit to the app's own account. The options that can plausibly reach $1,000 in one transaction on roughly three months of income history are a Texas pawn loan (collateral-based, no credit or income check), a co-signed personal loan (Upgrade is the most consistently cited lender), Oportun (no minimum score, Texas listed), a 401(k) loan if a vested balance exists, and, for 1099 workers, Giggle Finance; EarnIn and MoneyLion Instacash can reach $1,000 across a pay period for established users but not in a single day for new ones.

### Inferences
- A W-2 borrower at about $5,300-$6,000/month with three months of deposits is unlikely to get $1,000 from one app on day one. The fastest app path to $1,000 is stacking: e.g., Chime MyPay (up to $500 after 1-2 direct deposits into Chime) plus EarnIn (roughly $85-$150/day building to as much as $750-$1,000 per pay period) plus a second app's starter limit. This requires routing direct deposit to Chime and/or MoneyLion, which takes at least one to two pay cycles to unlock, so it is not same-week money for a brand-new user.
- EarnIn is the only app that works against an external bank account and credibly reaches $750-$1,000 within one pay period, but at $85-$150 per day it takes about 7-12 days of daily cash-outs to accumulate $1,000.
- The Fundbox 600-score floor excludes this borrower regardless of time in business. Lendio's general 520 floor and six-month/$8,000-per-month figures suggest a contractor with three months of revenue at about $5,300-$6,000/month would not meet typical marketplace minimums. Giggle Finance is the only 1099 option found that plausibly accepts three months of deposit history, and only if the "no minimum credit" sources rather than Finder's listing are correct.
- Fig Loans' "3 months of direct deposits, $1,400/month" rule matches this borrower's profile exactly, but its Texas maximum may fall short of $1,000.
- Pawn is the only option where neither credit nor income history matters; the loan size is limited purely by collateral value (a $1,000 loan needs an item the pawnshop values well above $1,000).

### Gaps
- No source found for Current's Paycheck Advance, Atlas, or PayPal/Venmo cash-borrowing products; their 2026 limits, fees and Texas availability are unverified.
- No official list of states for Chime Instant Loans was found, so Texas availability is unconfirmed.
- No source confirmed whether Oportun accepts co-signers in 2026, or its Texas-specific minimum income/history rule.
- No source found on a months-of-revenue minimum for Giggle Finance or on whether any merchant cash advance provider accepts a sole proprietor with three months of revenue.
- No source found on how many pay cycles of history EarnIn, Dave, Brigit, Klover, Albert or Cleo require before reaching their maximum limits; only the "new users start lower" statements above.
- Employer paycheck advances (direct from the employer, outside DailyPay/Payactiv): no source found; this is employer-policy-specific and would have to be confirmed with HR.
- Whether the borrower's 401(k) plan allows loans, has a new-hire waiting period, or has any vested balance after about three months is plan-specific and unknown.

## What are the true costs (fees, tips, express fees, effective APR) of each?

### Takeaway
Cash advance apps look cheap in dollars ($2-$9 instant fees, $5-$16 monthly subscriptions, optional tips) but short repayment windows turn those into high effective APRs; the flat-fee small lenders (Possible, Fig) run roughly 100%-250% APR, while Oportun, Chime Instant Loans and co-signed loans sit at or below about 36%. Free or near-free paths exist if the borrower can wait 1-5 business days (EarnIn, MoneyLion, Chime MyPay standard delivery).

### Inferences
- (Own arithmetic, simple annualization = fee / principal x 365 / days.) EarnIn $150 cash-out with a $5.99 Lightning fee and no tip, repaid in 14 days: about 104% APR; repaid in 7 days: about 208%. Seven such daily cash-outs to reach about $1,000 would cost about $42 in instant fees, or $0 with standard delivery.
- Cash App Borrow 5% for four weeks is about 65% APR; on $400 the fee is $20.
- Varo Advance $40 on $500: 8% flat; if repaid in 30 days that is about 97% APR, about 195% over 15 days. Varo Line of Credit $500 fee on $2,000 is a 25% flat charge over up to 12 months of amortizing payments, roughly 45% APR by rule of thumb (not a disclosed APR).
- Chime MyPay $500 with a $2-$5 instant fee over 14 days is about 10%-26% APR, and 0% with standard delivery, making it the cheapest app option found for an eligible Chime member.
- Dave at 5% on $500 ($25, though one source describes the fee as $5-$15) over 14 days would be about 130% APR.
- Subscription apps (Brigit, Albert, Cleo, Tilt) cost the monthly fee whether or not an advance is taken; a $250 advance on a $14.99 plan held 14 days is about 156% APR before any instant fee.
- Cost ranking for $1,000 over about one month, cheapest to dearest: employer advance / 401(k) loan / app standard-delivery advances (about $0); co-signed loan, Oportun or Chime Instant Loan (about 36% APR, roughly $30 interest for one month, plus any Oportun origination fee of $30-$100); Cash App Borrow (about 65%); Texas pawn (180%, $150); Possible/Fig (about 100%-250%).

### Gaps
- No reliable source found for a measured average APR on earned wage access (figures such as "over 100%" circulate, but the search returned no primary CFPB report text); do not cite a specific average.
- Giggle Finance factor rates are unpublished, so its true APR cannot be stated.
- No source found for Texas-specific Possible Finance fee schedule (CAB fee plus lender interest) or Fig's Texas APR.
- Dave's 2026 fee structure remains unresolved across sources.
- No source found for typical share-secured loan rates at credit unions in 2026 or for secured-card cash advance fees and APRs.

Eligibility for nearly all of them turns on a linked bank account showing recurring direct deposits rather than a credit score.

## Which options also help rebuild credit so the borrower qualifies for cheaper loans later?

### Takeaway
Cash advance apps and pawn loans do not build credit; the credit-building tools are products that report installment or revolving payments (Self credit-builder loan, Kikoff, installment lenders such as Oportun, Possible and Fig, a co-signed loan, and secured products). Kikoff and Self do not put spendable cash in hand up front, so they complement rather than replace the $1,000 loan.

### Cited Findings
- Kikoff: monthly membership rather than interest, with plans at about $5, $20 or $35 per month as of July 2026 (Basic $5/month billed at $60/year); the credit line can only be used in Kikoff's own store, so it provides no cash. — [FirstCard Kikoff credit account](https://www.firstcard.app/learn/kikoff-credit-account); [Gerald Kikoff](https://joingerald.com/learn/debt--credit/kikoff-lending)
- Self credit-builder loan: payments go into a savings account and the loan amount is released at the end of the term; from about $25 a month; interest cost is real. Reporting claims conflict (all three bureaus versus only TransUnion and Equifax). — [FirstCard apps like Kikoff](https://www.firstcard.app/learn/apps-like-kikoff); [Gerald](https://joingerald.com/learn/debt--credit/costs-credit-building-apps-first-borrowers)
- Co-signed loans affect both parties' credit; missed payments hurt both scores. — [Experian](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/can-you-get-a-personal-loan-with-a-cosigner/)
- Tilt offers a line of credit up to $1,000 with fixed monthly payments, separate from its cash advance. — [MoneyLion Empower/Tilt review](https://www.moneylion.com/learn/personal-loans/reviews/empower-app)

### Inferences
- A two-track approach is implied: get the $1,000 from the cheapest non-credit source available (app stacking, pawn, co-signer, Oportun), and in parallel open one low-cost reporting product (Kikoff at about $5/month or a Self loan at about $25/month) so that after 6-12 months of on-time history the borrower may clear the roughly 580-600 thresholds used by mainstream bad-credit lenders.
- Self functions as forced savings: the borrower gets the principal back at term end, so it also builds the cash cushion needed for a later share-secured loan.

### Gaps
- No 2026 source retrieved confirming that Possible Finance, Fig Loans, Oportun, Varo Line of Credit, Tilt's line of credit or Chime Instant Loans report to credit bureaus, or to which bureaus. These should be verified before the report states they build credit.
- No source found on share-secured or savings-secured loans (typical rate spread over the share dividend rate, percent of savings lendable, Texas credit unions offering them) or on secured credit cards with cash advance features.
- No source found on whether EWA/cash advance apps report (they are generally described as non-reporting, but this was not sourced here).

## What are the regulated costs of a Texas pawn loan of about $1,000?

### Takeaway
For loans made July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027, a Texas pawn loan between $276.01 and $1,840.00 is capped at 180% APR, which is 15% per month, so a $1,000 one-month pawn loan carries a maximum pawn service charge of about $150 (repay about $1,150). Early redemption triggers a pro-rated refund of the charge above $15.

### Cited Findings
- OCCC one-month pawn rate chart, effective July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027: $0.01-$276.00 at 240% APR; $276.01-$1,840.00 at 180% APR; $1,840.01-$2,760.00 at 30% APR; $2,760.01-$23,000.00 at 12% APR. — [OCCC PawnRateChart2027](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/PawnRateChart2027.pdf)
- The OCCC's February 4, 2026 notice set the Section 371.159 bracket amounts at $276.00, $1,840.00 and $2,760.00 for loans made on or after July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027; brackets are adjusted yearly for CPI. — [OCCC notice 02-04-2026](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/02-04-2026.pdf); [OCCC interest rates page](https://www.occc.texas.gov/publications/interest-rates)
- The chart's example shows a $360 loan with a one-month charge of $54.00 (15%), indicating the bracket rate applies to the whole loan amount. — [OCCC PawnRateChart2027](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/PawnRateChart2027.pdf)
- "Any pawn service charge (PSC) exceeding $15 is subject to refund" on early redemption of a one-month loan; the amount above $15 is reduced by one-thirtieth of the total pawn service charge for each day between redemption and the original maturity date. — [OCCC PawnRateChart2027](https://occc.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/PawnRateChart2027.pdf); [Tex. Fin. Code 371.159](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._fin._code_section_371.159)
- A pawnbroker who charges more than the authorized amount is liable for twice the pawn service charge contracted for. — [Tex. Fin. Code 371.306](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/tx/FI/371.306)
- The OCCC published a pawnshop rule review on April 3, 2026 (comments through May 3, 2026); the outcome was not found. — [OCCC rules page](https://occc.texas.gov/publications/legal/rules/)

### Inferences
- Maximum cost of a $1,000 Texas pawn loan: about $150 per 30-day period (15%), i.e. $300 if held two months via extension/renewal and $450 for three. Redeeming after 15 days would refund roughly half of the $150 charge (15/30 x $150 = $75 refunded, about $75 paid), subject to the $15 minimum.
- The top bracket's $23,000 upper bound appears to be the maximum pawn loan amount for the period.
- A pawn loan carries no credit check, no income verification and no collections or credit damage on default (the only consequence is forfeiting the item), which makes it the one option here whose availability is independent of both score and length of income history. It does not help rebuild credit.
- Just above $1,840 the cap falls from 180% to 30% APR, so shops have little incentive to lend in the $1,840-$2,760 band; a $1,000 loan sits squarely in the expensive bracket.

### Gaps
- The OCCC chart PDF and the statute text could not be opened directly in this session; the bracket figures come from search-result extraction of the OCCC PDF and should be confirmed against the document before publication.
- Not sourced here: the statutory maximum pawn loan term (one month) and the grace period before forfeiture, lost-ticket and other ancillary fees, and whether the 15% charge can be exceeded by any other permitted fee. These need the text of Tex. Fin. Code Chapter 371 and 7 Tex. Admin. Code Chapter 85.
- No data found on typical loan-to-value ratios at Texas pawnshops (what collateral value is needed to borrow $1,000).
